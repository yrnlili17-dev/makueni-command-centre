1. Grassroots Socioeconomic EmpowermentCandidates focus heavily on direct poverty alleviation and financial support within their constituencies.NG-CDF Bursaries: Prioritizing education by directly subsidizing school fees and tertiary education for disadvantaged students.Wealth & Group Support: Direct financial injection into local women's groups, youth bodaboda (motorcycle) associations, and table-banking initiatives.Welfare Contributions: Assisting constituents with personal expenses, particularly medical bills and funeral (harambee) contributions.2. Infrastructural & Constituency DevelopmentVoters expect MPs to directly manage and distribute local development funds to address community needs.Education Infrastructure: Funding the construction of new classrooms, laboratories, and school dining halls via NG-CDF.Local Amenities: Investing in access to clean water (boreholes), rural electrification, and local market sheds.Feeder Roads: Lobbying the national government and county roads boards to grade, gravel, or tarmac local access roads.3. Execution of Constitutional Mandates While voters prioritize local development, formal campaign manifestos emphasize an MP's three core national duties:Legislative Action: Proposing and voting on national bills and tax rates.Oversight: Checking the national government and ensuring equitable national budget allocation to specific regions.Representation: Voicing constituent concerns, such as agricultural reforms or local land policies, in the National Assembly.4. Political Patronage & Party AlignmentWinning a parliamentary seat heavily relies on political networks, tribal/ethnic alliances, and party backing.Coalition Politics: Aligning with the ruling coalition or major opposition parties to ensure the constituency receives government favor.Local Opinion Leaders: Mobilizing community elders, religious leaders, and business networks to build a reliable voting base.
